FXcursion · interface ↔ audio · SPI1

One Wire,
Two Streams

The recorder and the looper share a single SPI link that has no sync word, no length and no CRC. Everything that looks over-engineered in this code exists because of that one fact. Here is the whole protocol, and an honest list of what could be simpler.

The code looks bloated in places, and some of it was. Five findings are marked below; two have been fixed — including a defect that would have overrun a buffer by five times the first time a loop was saved — and three are judgement calls left in place with the reasoning recorded. Most of the remaining bulk is one constraint pushing outward, and it is worth seeing that constraint clearly before deciding what to cut.

01The constraint everything follows from

SPI1 carries a continuous, positionally framed stream. The audio board sends one frame per audio block, forever. The interface knows which sample belongs to which channel purely by counting — slot 0 is channel 0 because it is the first word to arrive, not because anything says so.

There is no sync word to resynchronise on, no length to validate, no CRC to fail. That makes it fast, and it makes it fragile in exactly one way:

Lose a single 32-bit word and every slot is permanently rotated.
The stream keeps flowing. The data keeps looking like audio.
Every channel is in the wrong file from that moment on, and
nothing in the payload can reveal it.

That is not a hypothetical. It is why three things in this design that each look like overkill are not:

  • The receive DMA is armed once and never re-armed — the gap between two transfers is precisely where a word goes missing.
  • The interface arms its routing before the audio board is told to start, and the audio board is told to stop before the routing is torn down.
  • Loop data cannot have its own SPI transaction, because starting one would mean re-arming. It has to ride inside the recorder's frames.

The frame is REC_SLOT_QTY recorder slots followed by nSlotQty loop slots. When no loop is moving the frame is four slots wide; during a transfer it widens to sixteen, and narrows again when the transfer ends.

Sixteen, and not any number you like. The receiver's ring is a fixed count of words and its half-transfer interrupt fires at the halfway word, so the total width has to divide that half exactly — otherwise the boundary lands mid-frame and everything after it is rotated. At 4096 words per half the legal widths are 8, 16 and 32. Sixteen is the middle one.

The two halves are read completely differently, and that difference explains most of the code:

The same wire, two meanings
 Recorder slotsLoop slots
what a slot is a channel. Slot 2 is always channel 2. four bytes of an undifferentiated byte stream.
payload per slot 3 bytes — a 24-bit sample, top byte padding 4 bytes — every byte used
why the difference the receiver de-interleaves by position, so a sample must sit in a known slot one contiguous run; sample boundaries inside it matter to nothing until the WAV header is written
destination four separate SDRAM planes one contiguous staging buffer
MDMA routes 4 — one per plane 1 — the run is lifted whole
slots per frame 4, always 12, only while a transfer runs

That last row is load-bearing. The MDMA offers five routes — one channel plus four linked-list nodes. Four are spoken for by the recorder planes. Sixteen loop slots as sixteen routes would not have fitted; as one contiguous run, it does. That is why the loop slots are grouped at the end of the frame rather than interleaved among the recorder's.

04What each piece is for

You said you don't know why loop_xfer has the functions it has, or what a "spool" is. Here is every module on the path, and the one job it exists to do.

Audio controller — the sender

Modules on the audio side
ModuleIts one jobWhy it is separate
rec_stream.c Hold two staged frames — one going out, one being filled. The DMA reads one while the audio block writes the other. Without two, a block would be written into the buffer being transmitted, splicing two blocks into one.
rec_spi.c Ask how wide the frame is, stage the recorder samples, let the loop fill the rest, hand it to the transport. It is the only place that knows about both streams. Everything either side of it knows about exactly one.
loop_xfer.c Turn a loop in SDRAM into bytes in slots, and back. This is the answer to "why does it have those functions". It is a codec, not a manager: OnOpen and OnCtl answer the two protocol messages, StreamWidth reports how wide the frame must be, Block moves one block's worth. Four entry points, one per thing that can happen to it.
loop_mem.c Own the loop buffers and a one-block DTCM window onto them. The window survives because the loop wraps: a block straddling the loop end is two runs in memory, and every caller handling that itself is how a click at the loop point appears at some lengths and not others.
SPI_TP Own the peripheral, the clock and the DMA. The link speed lives in spi_tp_cfg.h, not in whatever CubeMX last generated.

The burst must finish inside the block, or the next block has nowhere to go and is dropped — silently, in both the recording and the loop. That is the entire reason for 96 MHz rather than 48:

frame width          48 MHz    96 MHz   share of the 1333 µs block
 4  recorder only       13%        6%     divides the half
 8  4+4 loop            26%       13%     divides the half
16  4+12 loop           51%       26%     divides the half — in use
32  4+28 loop          102%       51%     divides, but 48 MHz cannot fit it

51% sustained for the seconds a transfer lasts is not a margin, it is a cliff — and it coincides with the DSP at full tilt and both SDRAM banks serving a looper alongside delay or reverb. At 96 MHz the same frame leaves three quarters of every block free.

Notice that the width column is not a free choice: only 8, 16 and 32 appear because those are the widths that divide the receiver's 4096-word half. 20 and 24 are missing because they do not, and a frame that does not divide puts the half-transfer interrupt in the middle of a frame.

06Saving a loop, in order

Nine steps. The ordering constraints are the interesting part — three of them are not negotiable.

GUI        LoopSession_StartSave(looper, planes, name)
             │
  1          ├─ LoopSpool_Acquire()          take slot A or B, or refuse
  2          ├─ Recorder_ArmLoopDest()       ← ARM BEFORE ASKING
  3          └─ LOOP_OPEN ────────────────────────────────────▶ UART
                                                     │
  4                          LoopXfer_OnOpen()  ◀─┘  how long is the take?
  5          ◀──────────────────────────────── LOOP_STAT(READY, bytes, slots)
             │
  6          ├─ FxLoop_OpenReply()           both sides now agree on the size
  7          └─ LOOP_CTL(START) ──────────────────────────────▶
                                                     │
  8                            the frame widens; blocks flow
             ◀════════════ MOSI, 12 loop slots per frame ═════════
             │
  9          ◀──────────────────────────────── LOOP_STAT(COMPLETE, crc)
             │
             └─ LoopSession_Poll()
                 ├─ wait for both: bytes landed AND peer reported
                 ├─ Crc32_Ieee(staging) == peer crc ?
                 ├─ Recorder_DisarmLoopDest()
                 └─ LoopSpool_Commit()  → queued for the card

The three orderings that matter

Arm before asking (step 2). The route is in place before any possibility of a block. A block arriving with nothing routed is not recoverable — there is no framing to notice it with, and the loop would simply be missing bytes from its middle.

Disarm before narrowing (step 9). The reverse. While the route is armed it keeps writing whatever the wire leaves in slots the audio board has already stopped filling.

Wait for both conditions. The interface knows its byte count is satisfied; the audio board knows it sent everything. Waiting on the local count alone would run the CRC comparison against a value that had not arrived yet. Waiting on the report alone would checksum a staging slot the MDMA had not finished filling.

R1 could be simpler

Two modules where one might do

LoopSpool and LoopSession are separate because one owns the card and the other owns the wire. That is a real seam — the spool's queue and recorder-priority logic have nothing to do with the protocol — but it costs two headers, two init calls and a shared nSlot that both touch.

If you merge them, you lose the ability to test the card side without a link, which is currently possible. I would keep the split, but the criticism is fair and the cost is real. If it stays, nSlot ownership should move entirely into the spool with the session asking for it by handle.

07The card, and who gets it

The recorder writes four WAVs continuously. A loop save wants the same card. A card handles one sequential stream far better than two interleaved, so they take turns — and the turn order is deliberately not first-come.

loop A writes  ──────────────▶ done
                                 │
recorder waits, ring filling ────┤  backlog: ~3 MB of a 50 MB ring
                                 ▼
                          RECORDER GOES NEXT   even though loop B is queued
                                 │
                        catches up at ~4× real time
                                 │
                                 ▼
loop B starts immediately  ────────────────────▶

LoopSpool_IsBusy() stays TRUE across all of it.
It never blinks false in the gaps.

The recorder goes next because it has been locked out for seconds and is carrying a backlog. Putting a second loop ahead of it is how a backlog becomes an overrun, and overrun audio is gone. The loop is already safely in SDRAM; the recorder's backlog is not.

The busy flag staying true is what makes the "saving" indicator honest, and it is also the gate: LoopSpool_Load returns RESULT_BUSY during that whole window rather than queueing, because a read starting mid-recovery locks the recorder out a second time before it has cleared the first.

R2 worth measuring

The CRC and the packing both sit in the audio block

Per block, with a transfer running, the audio side spends roughly 2.6–5% of its budget on the CRC over 4096 loop bytes, plus the window copies. That is affordable. It is listed because it is invisible — nothing reports it, and it only appears when a loop transfer coincides with a heavy patch.

Worth doing: put the block's worst-case cycle count on the debug UART. You already have DWT->CYCCNT sampled in ProcessHalf.

08Review findings

Five things. R3 and R4 are fixed — they are kept here rather than deleted, because a fix with no record of what it fixed is how the same mistake gets made twice. R5 to R7 are judgement calls where the current shape is defensible but the cost is real.

R3 defect · fixed

The receive ring was sized for a four-slot frame only

The ring is REC_RX_WORDS = 8192 words, and the de-interleave was told REC_RX_HALF_FRAMES = 1024 frames unconditionally. Correct at four slots per frame, wrong at every other width:

half = 4096 words

 at  4 slots/frame  →  1024.0 frames   OK
 at 20 slots/frame  →   204.8 frames   not even a whole number

it would have read 1024 × 20 × 4 = 81,920 B
from a half that holds 16,384 B

A five-times overrun into whatever follows the ring, the first time a loop was saved. LoopSession_FillTx's half offset had the same bug.

What changed. Frames-per-half is now derived from the width, the width must divide the half, and an illegal width is refused rather than clamped:

REC_RX_HALF_WORDS       (REC_RX_WORDS / 2)
REC_FRAMES_PER_HALF(w)  (REC_RX_HALF_WORDS / (w))
REC_WIDTH_IS_LEGAL(w)   (REC_RX_HALF_WORDS % (w) == 0)

4096 divides by 8, 16 and 32 but not by 20, so FX_LOOP_SLOT_QTY_MAX went from 16 to 12 — total width 16, 256 frames per half. It costs 2.5 s per loop instead of 1.88.

Two static asserts now pin the divisibility at build time, and MDMA_Trigger_Deinterleave refuses an ACKed width it cannot de-interleave, counting it in recDeinterleaveRefused rather than recording plausible audio from the wrong channels.

R4 real cost · fixed

LoopXfer_At() did two divisions per payload byte

It converted a byte offset into a plane index and an offset within that plane, with a / and a %, once per byte:

4096 payload bytes per block  →  8192 divisions per block

at ~10 cycles each  =  81,920 cycles
block budget        = 639,840 cycles      ≈ 13%

Thirteen percent of the audio block spent on address arithmetic, for a pointer that almost always just advances by one. The plane boundary is crossed once in a multi-megabyte loop.

What changed. A walking pointer: pWalk and nWalkRemain, both advanced by one per byte, with the plane step taken only when the countdown reaches zero. The divide-and-modulo now happens once per block to re-derive the pointer from nByteOfs — which also means a session reset between blocks cannot leave a stale pointer behind.

divisions per block   8192  →  2

LoopXfer_At survives for the CRC span, where it runs at most twice per block.

R5 defensible · but you were right to notice

The routes are rebuilt from scratch every block

MDMA_Trigger_Deinterleave recomputes all five routes and runs FxInterleave_Xfer's full validation on each, every 1333 µs. Four of those five never change while the stream width is constant.

Why it is defensible: the validation is what refuses a geometry that would read past the frame, and it is cheap next to the transfer itself. Caching it means a cache to invalidate, and the thing that would invalidate it — a width change — is exactly the moment you least want a stale descriptor.

If you want it faster: compute once when the ACK arrives and again when the width changes, and keep a validity flag. The saving is real but small; the risk is a stale route, which is the failure this whole design is arranged to avoid. I would leave it.

R6 smell · justified, but flag it

RecStream_StageSlots() hands out a writable pointer

RecStream_Buffer returns const because nothing outside that module should write a staged block. StageSlots exists to break exactly that rule, so the loop transport can fill its slots in place.

The alternative is a second 5 KiB buffer and a copy per block. That is worse. But the const-breaking function is a genuine smell and should stay narrow: it is valid only for the half Stage just returned, and only before the transport starts. Nothing enforces that today.

Cheap hardening: have Stage return the writable pointer directly instead of an index, so there is no window in which a stale index can be passed.

R7 not bloat — keep

Things that look redundant and are not

Two staging halves on the audio side. One in flight, one filling. With one, a block would be written into the buffer the DMA is reading.

The DTCM window in loop_mem. Looks like a leftover from the PSRAM design. It is not: it handles the loop wrap in one place, and DTCM is zero-wait where SDRAM costs a row activate per sample.

Zeroing the loop slots when no transfer runs. Without it a finished transfer's last block stays in the staging buffer and the next frame carries stale audio in slots the interface is still routing.

Two CRCs. They are over different memories — the sender's looper and the receiver's staging. Comparing them is the only check on a link that carries no CRC of its own.
